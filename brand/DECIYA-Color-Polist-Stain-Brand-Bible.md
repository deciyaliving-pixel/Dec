| Judged | Only after the topcoat has dried/cured, in neutral light, on production pine |

### 2.1 The five-shade reference ladder

Color Polist is defined against Furniture Clinic Walnut water-based stain as the visual reference. When matching or briefing a supplier, place every candidate on this ladder. **Smoked Walnut sits between Medium Warm and Neutral Brown, sealed dead-matte.**

1. **Dark walnut** — deepest, lowest grain contrast (over-dark = rejected for hero).
2. **Medium warm walnut** — warm, grain-forward.
3. **Neutral brown walnut** — balanced, least warm cast. *Closest to the DECIYA lock.*
4. **Slightly reddish walnut** — off-brand unless explicitly briefed.
5. **Natural dead-matte walnut** — the *sheen* target applied to shades 2–3.

> Online swatches and shade charts lie: lighting, screen calibration, wood species, sanding grit and the final clear coat all shift the result. **Never approve a colour from a photo.**

## 4. Approved finishing systems (India, Sept 2026)

Color Polist is a **system**, not a brand loyalty. Any of the routes below is acceptable *only after a sample board passes Section 8.* Keep every layer inside one compatible coating family — **never mix water-based and solvent-based products in one container.**

| Route | Colour coat | Absorption control | Dead-matte topcoat | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Reference** | Furniture Clinic Walnut (water-based) | FC pre-stain conditioner | Water-based matte PU | Colour benchmark; imported cost acceptable |
| **B — Branded Indian (default)** | Asian Paints WoodTech Wood Stains Interior, Walnut | Compatible WoodTech sanding sealer / washcoat | **Asian Paints 1K PU Matt** (or Aquadur PU Interior Matt) | Mainstream production — the DECIYA default |
| **C — Economical** | MRF Interior Wood Stain, Walnut (solvent) | Compatible solvent washcoat | WoodTech Melamyne Matt *or* solvent 1K PU matt | Cost-led runs with a skilled sprayer |
| **D — Bulk trial** | Verified supplier stain (e.g. Gazraj 5 L) | Per supplier system | Verified matte PU | Price-sensitive batches — sample mandatory |

### 4.1 Topcoat verdict — 1K PU Matt vs Melamyne Matt over stained pine

**Default to 1K PU Matt.** On stained pine it gives a softer, more natural, genuinely dead-matte surface with better handling and moisture resistance. Melamyne Matt is harder, more lacquer-like and more likely to make blotching obvious — acceptable only for cost-led indoor work with an experienced spray finisher. Neither topcoat fixes uneven stain absorption; blotching is a *stain* problem solved at Section 6, not by the clear coat. For a hero-grade dead-matte walnut, 1K PU Matt wins.

### 4.2 Product distinctions (never conflate)

- **Penetrating stain** — colours the wood, leaves grain visible. Color Polist's colour layer.
- **Clear matte PU / varnish** — seals and protects, controls final sheen. Color Polist's topcoat.
- **Coloured/opaque polish or paint** — hides grain. **Never used** in Color Polist.

## 5. Colour-mixing formulas (starting tests only)

Mix **only within one brand, one product range and one compatible base.** Start light, add dark gradually. These are *starting points for a sample board, not guaranteed matches.*

| Formula (by volume) | Expected result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 100% Walnut | Baseline | Sample A control |
| 80% Walnut + 20% Natural/Oak | Lighter, grain-forward | Good for pale hero look |
| 70% Walnut + 20% Teak + 10% Mahogany | Warm, slightly richer | Watch the red-shift |
| 90% Walnut + 10% Dark Brown | Deeper neutral | Toward "dark walnut" |
| 80% Walnut + 15% Natural + 5% Black | Neutralised, de-warmed | **Black muddies fast — use sparingly** |
| 70% Walnut + 30% Natural | Noticeably lighter | For light-grain briefs |

**Rules:** ratios by volume; record ratio, batch number and application conditions for every mix; scale a *proven* test ratio linearly to the batch; a small amount of black quickly reads muddy/grey, so add it last and in tiny increments. Do **not** dose black to "fix" warmth without a sample.

## 6. Application SOP — pine (production standard)

Pine blotches because earlywood and latewood, and end grain, drink stain at different rates. The process — not the brand — delivers consistency.

**Locked sequence (default Route B, water-based):**

1. **Grade & inspect** pine. Kiln-dried only, moisture **8–12%**.
2. **Sand with the grain:** 80 → 120 → 180 pre-stain (finish 240 if smoother needed). Backboard corners **R8 round-over**; shelf edges **R2 chamfer**. Remove all dust.
3. **Control absorption:** one thin, compatible pre-stain conditioner *or* washcoat/sanding sealer. Light sand P220–P240 after sealer. Too much sealer = pale, flat colour — keep it thin.
4. **Apply Color Polist colour coat:** lint-free cloth, foam pad or spray, with the grain, consistent across the batch. Water-based dwell ~1–2 min, then wipe excess. Never let stain puddle and dry.
5. **Dry fully**, judge colour in neutral light. Add a **second thin coat** only if the sample is too light. (Solvent Route C: allow ~4–6 h before topcoat per label — never transfer one brand's drying time to another.)
6. **Dead-matte topcoat:** two thin, even coats of matte PU (1K PU Matt default). Respect recoat time. De-nib lightly P320–P400 **only if the TDS permits** — never sand through the colour.
7. **Cure** fully before packing. Do not judge sheen while wet — matte can look cloudy wet and clear on cure. Avoid wax/oil over the final coat (raises sheen).

**Blotch control:** identical dwell time and wiping pressure across every piece; test face grain and end grain separately; a second thin coat beats one over-wet coat.

**Safety:** ventilate; solvent systems need extraction, respirator and fire precautions; dispose of oily/solvent rags per local waste rules.

## 7. Quantity & coverage planning

Basis: all six faces finished, two colour coats, coverage **~12 m²/L** (Furniture Clinic reference: 500 ml ≈ 6 m²). Pine absorption allowance built in. **Arithmetic locked and corrected below.**

Surface area per rectangular piece: `SA = 2 × (L×W + L×T + W×T)`

| Piece size | Area/piece | Total (×500) | Stain, 2 coats +20% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 × 100 × 100 mm cube | 0.060 m² | **30.00 m²** | **~6.00 L** |
| 100 × 25 × 10 mm | 0.0075 m² | **3.75 m²** | **~0.75 L** |
| 100 × 50 × 20 mm | 0.016 m² | **8.00 m²** | **~1.60 L** |

> **Correction on record:** the 100×50×20 mm case is **8.00 m²** (2×(5000+2000+1000) mm² × 500), *not* 8.50 m². Use 8.00 m².

**Pine-specific allowance:** use **25–40%** for raw/absorbent pine (not the generic 20%) once you move off the reference coverage. Walnut hardwood would be 15–25%, teak 15–30% — but Color Polist runs on pine, so plan high and confirm on a weighed test panel before ordering the batch.

**Topcoat:** calculate separately from its own coverage — surface roughness and film build drive topcoat use, not stain absorption. Do **not** apply the pine stain allowance to the PU quantity.

**Practical purchase (round up to pack sizes):**

- 3.75 m² job → buy **1 L** stain.
- 8.00 m² job → buy **2 L** stain.
- 30.00 m² job → buy **6–7 L** stain (after a colour-approved production sample).
- Plus enough matte topcoat for **two thin coats**, sized from its own coverage.

## 8. Sample-board approval protocol (mandatory before any batch)

Never approve on one block — pine varies board to board. Minimum **six identical pine samples** from the *actual* production timber:

- Sand all identically; label **A–F**; record brand, shade, base, ratio, batch no.
- A: 100% Walnut · B: 80/20 Walnut/Natural · C: 70/20/10 Walnut/Teak/Mahogany · D: 90/10 Walnut/Dark Brown · E: 80/15/5 Walnut/Natural/Black · F: 70/30 Walnut/Natural.
- Identical dwell + wiping on every sample.
- Apply the **intended matte topcoat to all**.
- Compare **only after the clear coat has dried/cured**, in daylight *and* indoor light.
- Score: colour, undertone, grain visibility, end-grain darkening, sheen uniformity, blotching, touch, scratch/water resistance, blocking after stacking.
- **Pick the most uniform sample, not the darkest.** Approve the whole system, not just the colour.

If the six formulas are not chemically compatible with the chosen range, replace them with safe in-range formulas.

## 10. Quality control — Color Polist finish

**Reject immediately if:** orange/yellow cast · glossy or uneven sheen · sheen above 10% · visible blotching / zebra striping · dark end-grain patches · surface film or dried puddle marks · cross-grain sanding marks · sand-through on the colour coat · tacky/uncured surface · blocking after stacking.

**Per-unit finish checks (integrate with the 12-point Pegboard V2 QC):**

1. Colour matches the approved Smoked Walnut swatch — no orange.
2. Sheen matte, ≤10%, uniform across faces and edges.
3. Grain fully visible; no opaque coverage.
4. No blotch, streak or dark end grain.
5. Two even topcoat coats; no runs, no de-nib sand-through.
6. Fully cured; no tack, no blocking.

Every output is tested against: *premium perception · emotional trust · tactile authenticity · manufacturing consistency · long-term brand equity.* Fail any → reject and redo.

## 11. Packaging, photography & voice

**Packaging:** kraft wrap (recycled), pulp corner inserts, zero plastic on main components; outer kraft box, matte-black DECIYA stamp, minimal text; hardware in labelled kraft poly bags. The finish is protected in transit — no rub marks, which raise sheen.

**Photography:** DSLR, f/8, 4500–4800K, large softbox upper-left; warm off-white / limewash backdrop; max one plant + one ceramic + one neutral prop. **Preserve real wood grain, powder-coat texture and material shadows.** Never: CGI render, fake reflections, blue cast, over-sharpening, glossy simulation.

**Naming & voice:** "Color Polist — Smoked Walnut, dead-matte." Opinionated, exact, specced. Never call it paint. Never promise an "exact match" without a cured sample test.
